Mumbai Style Pav Bhaji

Description

PAV BHAJI is one of India's most iconic and beloved street foods, originating from the bustling streets of MUMBAI. It consists of a thick, vibrant, mashed vegetable curry (bhaji) cooked on a flat griddle with a signature blend of spices, laden with an incredibly generous amount of butter, and crowned with a layer of grated cheese. This spicy, tangy curry is served piping hot alongside pillowy soft dinner rolls (pav) that are shallow-fried to perfection in even more butter.

Pav Bhaji is much more than just a popular street snack-it is a fast-paced culinary phenomenon deeply woven into the daily hustle of Maharashtra. While it is celebrated globally for its rich, spicy, and buttery profile, the dish holds a fascinating history, a precise science of preparation, and a massive subculture of street-style variations.

Ingredients required to make Butter Loaded Pav Bhaji

  • 4 large potatoes, boiled and peeled
  • 1 cup green peas (fresh or frozen), boiled
  • 1/2 cup cauliflower florets, boiled
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 2 large tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1/2 cup capsicum (green bell pepper), finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2-3 tbsp Mumbai Pav Bhaji masala
  • 1 tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder (for vivid color)
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
  • 1/2 cup processed cheese, grated (for topping)
  • 4-5 tbsp salted butter (plus more for roasting pav)
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil
  • Salt to taste
  • 1/2 cup fresh coriander leaves, finely chopped
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges
  • 8-10 soft pav (dinner rolls)

STEPS

  1. Place the boiled potatoes, green peas, and cauliflower into a wide bowl and mash them thoroughly using a potato masher until smooth but slightly textured, then set aside
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil and 1 tablespoon of butter in a large, wide pan or flat tawa over medium heat
  3. Add the finely chopped onions and saut'e for 2 to 3 minutes until they turn translucent and soft
  4. Add the ginger-garlic paste and chopped capsicum, then saut'e for another 2 minutes until the raw aroma completely disappears
  5. Add the chopped tomatoes and a pinch of salt, cooking on medium-high heat until the tomatoes turn mushy and start releasing oil from the edges
  6. Lower the heat and stir in the Pav Bhaji masala, Kashmiri red chilli powder, and turmeric powder, mixing rapidly for 30 seconds to roast the spices
  7. Add the pre-mashed vegetables along with 1 cup of water, mixing everything together vigorously
  8. Use your potato masher directly inside the pan to mash the curry again on high heat, ensuring all the flavors fuse into a thick, uniform consistency
  9. Simmer the bhaji for 5 to 7 minutes on low heat, adjusting the consistency with a splash of water if it gets too thick, then stir in 2 tablespoons of butter and half of the chopped coriander
  10. Melt a generous tablespoon of butter on a separate hot tawa, slice your pav rolls horizontally in half, and toast them on both sides until they are golden-brown and pillowy soft
  11. Transfer the hot bhaji to a serving plate, heavily garnish the top with a dense layer of grated cheese, sprinkle the remaining fresh coriander, and serve immediately alongside the warm buttered pav and lemon wedges
